# Review request templates

Reviews are the single biggest free lever you have. They decide whether you appear in Google's
map results, and they decide whether someone who found you actually calls. A firm with twelve
reviews beats a firm with a better website and none.

## Before you send anything

**Get your review link.** In your Google Business Profile, open the listing, then find
"Ask for reviews". Google gives you a short link like `g.page/r/XXXXXXXX/review`. That link
opens the review box directly. Never send someone to "search for us on Google" — you lose most
people at that step.

Put the link in a note on your phone. You will use it constantly.

## The rules that make this work

1. **Ask by name and mention the specific matter.** "Your naturalisation" beats "your case".
2. **Ask when they are happiest** — the day an approval lands, not a month later.
3. **Ask everyone, not only the wins.** A client whose case is still pending can honestly
   review how you communicate.
4. **One follow-up, then stop.** Two asks is persistence. Three is pressure.
5. **Never offer anything in exchange.** Discounts or gifts for reviews break Google's rules
   and most bar advertising rules. A plain ask works better anyway.
6. **Never write a review yourself or ask staff to.** One fake review found is worse than
   fifty missing ones.

## WhatsApp — the main one

Most of your clients are already on WhatsApp. This is the version that will get used.

> Hi [Name] — congratulations again on the approval. 🎉
>
> Would you mind leaving a short Google review? It is the main way other families find us, and
> two sentences is plenty. Anything about how the process felt is useful.
>
> Here is the link: [REVIEW LINK]
>
> Thank you — and stay in touch.

**For a client whose case is still pending:**

> Hi [Name] — while we wait on [the petition / the interview], would you leave us a short
> Google review about how the process has gone so far? People choosing a lawyer really want to
> know what the communication is like, and you can speak to that better than we can.
>
> [REVIEW LINK]
>
> Two sentences is plenty. Thank you.

> other families find us. [REVIEW LINK] — Queenette, Hogan & Crown

## The one follow-up, five days later

> Hi [Name], just floating this back up in case it got buried. No pressure at all if you would
> rather not. [REVIEW LINK]

Then stop. Never ask a third time.

---

## In person, at the end of a matter

Say it out loud, then send the link while they are still sitting there:

> "One last thing — if you were happy with how this went, a Google review genuinely helps
> other families find us. I will send you the link right now so it is on your phone."

The send-it-immediately part is what makes this work. Sending it later almost never converts.

---

## Where to start today

You do not need new clients to start. Work through, in this order:

1. Every client whose matter closed in the last twelve months
2. Every current client, using the pending-case version
3. Colleagues and referring attorneys who can speak to your work

Ten reviews is the point where the listing starts to look established. Twenty-five is where it
starts to win. Send five requests a day for a fortnight and you are there.

## Reply to every review

Reply to all of them, good and bad, within a couple of days. Google counts engagement, and
people read replies more carefully than reviews.

**For a good review:**

> Thank you [Name]. It was a pleasure to act for you, and congratulations again.

**For a critical review** — never argue, never confirm they were a client, never discuss any
detail. Confidentiality survives a bad review.

> Thank you for the feedback. We take it seriously. Please call the office on 469-288-1606 so
> we can discuss it properly — we are not able to talk about any specific matter here.
