# 30 short-form video scripts

For TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts. One recording goes to all three.

**Before you post, read this.** These scripts cover settled, evergreen law. They avoid
fees, processing times, and visa bulletin dates, because those change. Check current
USCIS and State Department policy before you record. Review every script against your
own bar advertising rules. Add your disclaimer to the caption, not the spoken script,
because it wastes your first two seconds.

**How to shoot.** Phone at eye level. Face a window. Say the hook before you smile.
No intro, no "hi guys", no logo animation. The first two seconds decide everything.

**How to post.** Three a week. Same video to all three platforms. Reply to every comment
with a real answer, because the comments are where the clients come from.

## Section A — Visa denials and interviews

### 1. The real reason for a 214(b) denial

**Hook:** "Your visa wasn't denied because of your bank balance."

**Script:** Everyone thinks a bigger bank balance fixes a visa denial. It does not. Section
214(b) says the officer must assume you plan to stay in the United States forever. Your job
is to prove otherwise. Money does not prove that. Ties do. A job you are returning to. Property.
A business. School enrolment. A family that depends on you at home. People show up with a
fat statement and no proof of a life to return to, and they get denied in forty seconds.
Show the officer what you are coming back to.

**On-screen:** 214(b) = "we assume you'll stay" / your job is to prove you'll leave

**CTA:** "Denied under 214(b)? Comment 214 and I'll break down what to fix."

### 2. 221(g) is not a denial

**Hook:** "You got a 221(g). You have not been denied."

**Script:** A blue or white slip that says 221(g) is not a refusal. It means your case is
paused. Either a document is missing or your case is in administrative processing. Two
completely different situations. Missing document means send it and you move on. Administrative
processing means the consulate is doing extra checks and there is no deadline. The mistake
people make is starting a brand new application. Do not. You throw away your place in line
and your fee. Wait, or ask the right question in the right channel.

**On-screen:** 221(g) = paused, not refused

**CTA:** "Stuck in administrative processing? Tell me how long in the comments."

---

### 3. The one question that sinks student visa interviews

**Hook:** "This one question fails more student visas than any other."

**Script:** The officer asks: why this school? If you answer "it is a good school" or "my
agent chose it", you are done. That answer proves you did not choose a course, you chose a
country. Which is exactly what 214(b) is looking for. A strong answer names the programme,
one or two specific things about it, and how it connects to a job you can name back home.
Thirty seconds, specific, and it ends at home. Practice it out loud before you go.

**On-screen:** "Why this school?" is a 214(b) test in disguise

**CTA:** "Interview coming up? Comment your course and I'll tell you what they'll ask."

---

### 4. Do not lie about a previous denial

**Hook:** "Hiding an old visa denial is worse than the denial."

**Script:** People get refused, then apply again and tick "no" on the question about previous
refusals. They think it is a fresh start. It is not. Every application is in the system.
The officer sees it before you sit down. A denial is a normal thing that happens to millions
of people and it is not permanent. A false statement on a visa application is a different
category of problem entirely, and it can follow you for life. Tick yes. Explain what changed.
That is the answer that works.

**On-screen:** a denial expires / a misrepresentation doesn't

**CTA:** "Been refused before? It is fixable. Ask me how below."

### 5. Your interview starts before you sit down

**Hook:** "The officer decided about you before you opened your mouth."

**Script:** Consular officers do dozens of interviews a day, sometimes over a hundred. They
read your form first. By the time you reach the window they already have a view, and you
have about ninety seconds to confirm it or change it. So the work is in the form, not the
window. Every date consistent. Every name spelled the way it is on your passport. Employment
history with no unexplained gaps. Then at the window, short answers. Answer the question
they asked, not the question you practised.

**On-screen:** the form is the interview / the window is the confirmation

**CTA:** "Want the form checked before you submit? Link in bio."

### 6. The DS-160 mistake almost everyone makes

**Hook:** "One wrong box on the DS-160 costs you the whole appointment."

**Script:** People rush the DS-160 because it is long. Then they get to the window and the
officer reads back something they did not mean to say. The most common one is the purpose
of trip and the "person or organisation you will visit". People put a hotel, or a relative
whose status does not match the story, or leave it vague. That single field frames your
entire interview. Second most common is a name that does not match the passport exactly.
Read the whole form aloud once before you submit it. Once.

**On-screen:** read the DS-160 out loud before you submit

**CTA:** "Comment DS160 and I'll post the full checklist."

### 7. Dress code is not the point

**Hook:** "Stop asking what to wear to your visa interview."

**Script:** Nobody has ever been refused for wearing the wrong shirt. Look neat and stop
thinking about it. What actually decides it is whether your documents tell one story. If
you say you are a business owner, they want registration and account statements that move
like a business. If you say you are employed, they want a letter and a payslip pattern that
matches. If your papers say one thing and your mouth says another, that is the refusal. Spend
your energy on the story, not the outfit.

**On-screen:** one story, three sources, no contradiction

**CTA:** "What's the weirdest visa advice you've been given? Comments."

## Section B — Green cards and family

### 8. The 3 and 10 year bar is triggered by leaving

**Hook:** "Leaving the country is what triggers the ban. Not overstaying."

**Script:** This is the one that ruins lives. If you are in the United States past your
authorised stay, you build up what is called unlawful presence. More than a hundred and
eighty days and you get a three year bar. More than a year and you get ten. But here is the
part people miss. The bar does not switch on while you are inside. It switches on the moment
you depart. Which is why people who "go home to fix their papers properly" lock themselves
out for a decade. Talk to someone before you buy that ticket.

**On-screen:** the bar triggers on DEPARTURE

**CTA:** "Thinking of leaving to fix your status? Talk to me first. Free 15 minutes."

### 9. Marriage green card: they are not testing your love

**Hook:** "They don't care if you love each other."

**Script:** People prepare for the marriage interview like it is a romance quiz. What is her
favourite colour. Which side of the bed. That is not the test. The test is whether you
built a life together that leaves a paper trail. A joint lease. A joint account with real
movement in it, not one deposit made last week. Each other on insurance. Photos across
years and with both families, not twenty selfies from one weekend. Build the trail from
day one and the interview is ten minutes.

**On-screen:** they test the paper trail, not the feelings

**CTA:** "Comment MARRIAGE and I'll post the evidence checklist."

### 10. Do not travel on a pending green card

**Hook:** "Book that flight and you cancel your own green card application."

**Script:** If you filed to adjust status inside the United States and you leave the country
without advance parole, the government treats your application as abandoned. Not delayed.
Abandoned. You do not get a warning at the airport and the airline will not stop you. There
are exceptions, mostly for people holding valid H-1B or L status, and they are narrower than
people assume. So the rule is simple. Pending application plus international travel equals
ask first. Every time. Even for a funeral.

**On-screen:** pending I-485 + leaving = abandoned

**CTA:** "Need to travel while your case is pending? Ask me before you book."

### 11. The sponsor income question

**Hook:** "Your sponsor doesn't earn enough. Here's what actually happens."

**Script:** To sponsor a family member you sign an affidavit of support and promise your
income clears a set line above the federal poverty guideline for your household size. People
panic when they are under it. You have options. Assets can count, at a multiple of the
shortfall. A joint sponsor can step in and does not have to be a relative. Income from
household members who live with you can be added with the right form. Being under the line
is a problem to solve, not a wall. But you cannot solve it by guessing.

**On-screen:** under the line? assets, joint sponsor, household income

**CTA:** "Sponsoring family and worried about income? Book a call, link in bio."

### 12. K-1 or spouse visa

**Hook:** "Marry abroad or bring them here to marry? Pick wrong and you wait an extra year."

**Script:** The fiancé visa gets them into the country sooner. But it is only the first half.
Once they arrive you marry within ninety days and then file the whole green card application
from scratch, with another fee and another wait, and they cannot work for a while. The spouse
visa takes longer up front but they land as a permanent resident, or close to it, and they
can work immediately. So the real question is not which is faster. It is whether you need
them physically present sooner, or working sooner. Answer that and the choice is obvious.

**On-screen:** K-1 = here sooner / CR-1 = working sooner

**CTA:** "Which one fits you? Tell me your situation below."

### 13. Your green card is not permanent

**Hook:** "A green card can be lost. People find out at the airport."

**Script:** Permanent resident does not mean permanent no matter what. Long trips outside
the United States can be treated as abandoning your residence, and the officer at the border
makes that call. There is no magic number of days that keeps you safe, whatever your uncle
told you. If your life is clearly still based in the United States, you are usually fine.
If your job, your home, and your family are all somewhere else, you have a problem building.
Planning a long trip? There is a form for that. File it before you go, not after.

**On-screen:** long trips abroad can cost you your status

**CTA:** "Planning a long trip as a green card holder? Ask first."

## Section C — Work and study

### 14. Working on a student visa

**Hook:** "This 'small side job' can end your student visa."

**Script:** On an F-1 you have limited on-campus work rights during term. That is it, until
you get authorisation. Training options exist, both during your course and after, and they
have to be approved before you start, not after. Working for cash outside those rules is a
status violation. Not a small one. It can end your programme, follow you into every future
application, and it is the kind of thing that comes out years later at exactly the wrong
moment. If money is tight, there are legal routes. Ask about them before you take the job.

**On-screen:** authorisation comes BEFORE the job, never after

**CTA:** "Student and need to work? Comment F1 and I'll explain your options."

### 15. What employers get wrong about sponsorship

**Hook:** "Your boss says they 'don't do sponsorship'. They probably can."

**Script:** Most small employers say no to sponsorship because they think it means lawyers,
a fortune, and a year of paperwork. Often they are picturing the wrong category entirely.
Depending on your nationality, your role, and your qualifications, there may be a route that
is far lighter than what they are imagining. Some routes have no lottery. Some are specific
to certain countries. The conversation to have with your employer is not "will you sponsor
me". It is "can I bring you the specific option and what it costs". Bring the answer, not
the question.

**On-screen:** bring your employer the option, not the ask

**CTA:** "Employer said no to sponsorship? Comment WORK and I'll list the routes."

### 16. The lottery is not the only door

**Hook:** "You did not get picked in the lottery. There are other doors."

**Script:** Every year people treat the H-1B lottery like it is the only work visa in
existence. It is not. There are categories for people with extraordinary ability. There are
treaty-based categories tied to specific countries. There are intracompany transfers for
people already working for a company with a US office. There are routes through study and
training. Some of these have no cap and no lottery at all. The lottery is popular because it
is familiar, not because it is the best fit. Get your profile assessed against all of them.

**On-screen:** the lottery is one door, not the only one

**CTA:** "Not selected? Book a free 15 minute assessment, link in bio."

### 17. Employers: one paperwork habit that saves you

**Hook:** "This one file is why companies fail immigration audits."

**Script:** If you hire anyone in the United States you complete an employment eligibility
verification form for them. Simple. What sinks companies is where they keep it. Keep those
forms inside individual personnel files and an audit becomes a nightmare, because the
inspector now has a reason to open every file. Keep them in one separate binder, apart from
everything else, and an audit is a contained event. Same law, same forms, completely
different day. Fix it this week, not the week you get the notice.

**On-screen:** keep I-9s in a separate file, never in personnel files

**CTA:** "Business owner? Free compliance check, link in bio."

### 18. Changing jobs on a work visa

**Hook:** "You accepted the new job. Did you check your status first?"

**Script:** A work visa is usually tied to a specific employer, and sometimes to a specific
role and worksite. Resigning on Friday and starting somewhere new on Monday can leave a gap
where you have no status at all, even though you never stopped working. Some categories have
a grace period. Some let the new employer file and you start before approval. Some do not.
The order of operations matters more than the timing, and the offer letter is the wrong
moment to find that out. Check before you resign.

**On-screen:** check status BEFORE you resign

**CTA:** "Got an offer? Let's check the timing. Free call, link in bio."

## Section D — Enforcement and fear

### 19. If immigration knocks

**Hook:** "If they knock, do not open the door. Here is why."

**Script:** You do not have to open your door unless they have a warrant signed by a judge.
An administrative form signed by an immigration officer is not that. Ask them to slide it
under the door. Read the top. If it does not say a court and it is not signed by a judge,
you can say, through the closed door, that you do not consent to entry, and that you want to
speak to a lawyer. Then say nothing else. Not where you were born. Not how you entered.
Nothing. Silence is not rudeness. It is a right.

**On-screen:** judicial warrant = judge's signature + court name

**CTA:** "Save this. Send it to someone who needs it."

### 20. Do not sign anything

**Hook:** "They will hand you a paper. Do not sign it."

**Script:** If you are detained, someone will offer you a document and tell you signing gets
you out faster. Very often that document is you agreeing to leave the country and giving up
your right to see a judge. People sign it because they are frightened and because it is in
a language they read at speed under pressure. You have the right to say: I am not signing
anything until I speak with a lawyer. Say that sentence. Say it as many times as you need to.
Nothing bad happens because you waited.

**On-screen:** "I am not signing anything until I speak to a lawyer."

**CTA:** "Memorise that sentence. Teach it to your family."

### 21. A court date is not the end

**Hook:** "You got a notice to appear in immigration court. You have not lost."

**Script:** People see that document and start packing. Immigration court is a process, not
a verdict. Your first hearing is usually short and administrative, mostly about scheduling
and whether you have a lawyer. The case where you actually present your defence comes later,
and there are real defences. Relief for people with long residence. Asylum. Relief tied to
family members. Cancellation. But every one of them has to be raised properly and on time.
The one thing that guarantees you lose is not showing up. Go. Always go.

**On-screen:** missing a hearing = removal order in absentia

**CTA:** "Got an NTA? Do not wait. Book a call today."

### 22. The one-year asylum deadline

**Hook:** "The asylum clock is one year and it is already running."

**Script:** In general you have one year from arriving in the United States to file for
asylum. People wait because they are hoping the situation at home improves, or because they
are saving money, or because someone told them to wait for a lawyer. That year passes fast.
There are exceptions, for changed circumstances at home and for extraordinary circumstances
in your own life, and they are real but they are narrow and you have to prove them. If you
are near the line, filing something on time beats filing something perfect late.

**On-screen:** 1 year from arrival, exceptions are narrow

**CTA:** "Near the one year mark? Message me today, not next week."

### 23. Before you plead guilty to anything

**Hook:** "Your criminal lawyer may be about to end your immigration case."

**Script:** A plea that looks like a good deal in criminal court can be a permanent
immigration disaster. Certain offences make you deportable. Some make you permanently
ineligible for almost everything, even after you serve no time at all. Your criminal defence
lawyer is required to advise you about immigration consequences, but many are not specialists
and the advice is thin. Even a small charge, even a plea with no jail, deserves a second
opinion from an immigration lawyer before you sign. Before. Undoing a plea afterwards is
brutally hard.

**On-screen:** get an immigration opinion BEFORE you plead

**CTA:** "Facing a charge? Get the immigration opinion first. Link in bio."

## Section E — Citizenship

### 24. When you can apply for citizenship

**Hook:** "You might already be eligible for citizenship and not know it."

**Script:** The usual rule is five years as a permanent resident. Married to a US citizen and
living with them, it drops to three. But most people count the wrong way, because you can
file a little before you actually hit the date. People sit on an extra year for no reason.
There are other conditions: time physically inside the country, continuous residence, and
good moral character over the relevant period. Count your dates properly. Then check whether
anything in the last few years affects that character test before you file.

**On-screen:** 5 years, or 3 if married to a USC

**CTA:** "Not sure if you're eligible yet? Comment your green card year."

---

### 25. The citizenship question that catches people

**Hook:** "The naturalisation form asks about things you thought were forgotten."

**Script:** The application asks whether you have ever been arrested, cited, or detained. Ever.
Anywhere. Including things that were dismissed. Including things that never went to court.
Including things from before you got your green card that nobody asked about at the time.
People tick no because it feels finished, and it is not finished, because the background
check goes deeper than the green card check did. Disclosing an old dismissed matter is
almost always survivable. Concealing it is what puts your status itself at risk.

**On-screen:** "ever arrested" means ever, including dismissed

**CTA:** "Something in your past? Let's check it before you file, not after."

### 26. Do not travel right before your interview

**Hook:** "Do not book that trip before your citizenship interview."

**Script:** Two things go wrong. First, continuous residence. A long trip in the wrong window
can reset a clock you have almost finished running, and people find this out at the interview.
Second, plain logistics. Interview notices arrive with modest notice and missing one is not
free. If you have a case pending, keep your trips short and keep your address updated with
the agency, which is a legal obligation with a deadline anyway and one that almost nobody
follows. Move house, update it within the deadline.

**On-screen:** update your address within the legal deadline

**CTA:** "Case pending and need to travel? Ask me first."

## Section F — Scams and trust

### 27. Notario fraud

**Hook:** "In Nigeria a notary is a lawyer. In America they are not."

**Script:** This is why the same scam works over and over on immigrant families. In many
countries a notary is a qualified legal professional. In the United States a notary public
is someone licensed to watch you sign a document. That is the entire job. They cannot give
you legal advice. They cannot represent you before immigration. Only a licensed attorney or
a specifically accredited representative can. People pay thousands to notarios, get forms
filed wrong, and end up in removal proceedings. Ask for a bar number. A real lawyer will
give it to you in one second.

**On-screen:** ask for the bar number. always.

**CTA:** "Ask me for mine. I'll give it to you right now."

### 28. The lottery is free

**Hook:** "If someone charged you to enter the visa lottery, you were robbed."

**Script:** Entering the diversity visa lottery is free. There is one official government
site and it costs nothing to submit. Every agent charging you a fee to enter is selling you
something you can do yourself in fifteen minutes. Worse, many of them enter on your behalf
with their own contact details, so if you are selected, they hold your result hostage and
charge you again. And the results are checked on the same free official site. Nobody emails
you to say you won. Nobody. If you got that email, it is a scam.

**On-screen:** free to enter. results checked online. nobody emails you.

**CTA:** "Share this with someone about to pay an agent."

---

### 29. How the government contacts you

**Hook:** "Immigration will never call you and ask for a gift card."

**Script:** Real notices arrive by post, on paper, with a case number you can check yourself
online. The government does not phone you demanding immediate payment. It does not accept
gift cards, wire transfers to a person, or crypto. It does not threaten you with arrest in
an hour unless you pay right now. That call is a scam and it targets immigrants specifically
because the fear is real and the caller knows it. Hang up. Then check your case status
yourself on the official site. Then tell your family, because they get the same call.

**On-screen:** post + case number = real / phone + payment = scam

**CTA:** "Send this to your parents. They get these calls."

### 30. What a real consultation looks like

**Hook:** "If a lawyer promises you a result, walk out."

**Script:** Nobody can guarantee an immigration outcome. Not me, not anyone. The officer
decides. What a real consultation gives you is different: a clear read of where you actually
stand, the options that genuinely apply to you, the risks of each one said out loud, and a
written fee agreement before any money moves. If someone quotes a price before they have
heard your history, they are selling a form, not advice. Ask what could go wrong. A good
lawyer will have a longer answer than you expected.

**On-screen:** no guarantees. written fees. risks said out loud.

**CTA:** "Free 15 minutes, no pitch. Link in bio."
